REalyse Pulse · Real Estate Market intelligence platform

Pulse reached 73% user adoption in the first quarter after a 5-month 0→1 launch.

Real estate teams could read the market in one place instead of stitching five tools together. REalyse got a flagship platform in Q3 2024, and a foundation to grow the product and the design team.

03 · The problem

Professionals spent 8+ hours a week consolidating market data.

Developers, lenders, and investors needed to know where, when, and what to build or buy. The data existed. It lived in separate tools, with no shared picture across analyst, manager, and executive. Switching created delay, and delay created missed deals.

8+ hrs Weekly time users described spending just to assemble a market picture from multiple sources.
4 roles Analysts, managers, executives, and adjacent stakeholders needed different views of the same underlying data, not one dashboard.

The failure was specific. A feature-rich intelligence product still fails if people cannot trust the number, and if they cannot see the comparable properties behind a valuation. More charts without provenance would have been another tab to close.

  • Market data was fragmented across tools, so context died every time someone switched.
  • Teams asked for unified intelligence they could reach in minutes, not another export.
  • A one-size-fits-all dashboard would ignore how an analyst, a portfolio manager, and an executive actually decide.

04 · Research & discovery

Developers, lenders, and consultants already knew exactly where things got stuck.

I ran the research with go-to-market and product so the opportunity was not a designer’s hunch. A survey of 32 people mapped the stall at scale. Five interviews with developers, lenders, and consultants made the friction specific. Competitive work across five platforms showed where others were weak on integration and role-based views. Four role sketches helped the team talk, but they were bets, not finished truths.

01 · Speed needs alerts

Real-time market alerts and trend detection changed how fast people would act, and how sure they felt.

02 · Trust beats features

Users valued reliable, transparent data more than a long feature list. They needed a signal they could believe.

03 · One dashboard is not four jobs

Analysts, managers, and executives needed different cuts of the same data. A shared dump would serve none of them.

04 · Consolidation is the product

The takeaway was blunt: disconnected data slows decisions. People asked for one place, in minutes.

Hypothesis · Market analyst

Needs filters, export, and templates to turn dense data into a report someone else can act on.

Hypothesis · Portfolio manager

Needs alerts, trends, and a dashboard that tracks the book against the market, not a research workbench.

Hypothesis · Executive

Needs a two-minute briefing: KPIs, portfolio health, macro, instead of hours of consolidation.

These were alignment tools. Tests later proved which flows actually mattered, especially comparables.

05 · The key insight

When people value a property, comparables are the real work.

Low-fi tests with 6 users made this sharp. Matching size, condition, and featuresFinding comparable properties in the same area was the step that made a REalyse valuation believable. It had to be visual, and people had to be able to check each property themselves. Unified data and role-based views still mattered, but they were how we got to the comparable, not a replacement for it.

06 · Process

Low-fidelity wireframes and user testing

I partnered closely with engineering and data science to prototype core user flows. Using low-fidelity wireframes, we validated information architecture and core interactions with 6 test users before committing to high-fidelity design. That sped up decisions and cut rework later in development.

Key sessions tested: information hierarchy and dashboard layouts for different roles, mental models for data filtering and exploration, alert notification patterns and frequency, and compare and benchmark workflows.

Key insight from testing. When people assess property value, they rely on comparable home prices: matching size, condition, and features in the same area. Identifying comparables proved to be the most critical step in generating a REalyse valuation report. It needed to be visual, and users wanted to verify the characteristics of each property.

High-fidelity design and design system

Real-time market alerts and trend detection. Built on user testing, I designed dashboards so portfolio managers see investment-focused metrics and alerts, analysts see export and filtering tools, and executives see high-level KPIs and portfolio health, all from the same underlying data.

Finding 1 in action. Real-time market alerts and trend detection significantly improve the speed and confidence of investment decisions.

Unified data intelligence. I designed data exploration to surface market insights without leaving the platform: advanced filtering, comparative analysis, and trend visualization. Transparent data provenance indicators build confidence in recommendations.

Findings 1 and 2 in action. Unified data access cuts tool switching, while data transparency builds decision confidence. Users spend 60% less time on data consolidation than in previous workflows.

Market alerts and real-time intelligence. Building on Finding 3, I designed an alert system that surfaces market opportunities and anomalies in real time. Users set thresholds and get notified when conditions match their criteria.

Finding 3 in action. Real-time alerts surface opportunities earlier. Early adopters reported capturing 3x more investment opportunities in the first month after launch.

Design system and team scaling. To support iteration after launch, I built a design system documenting component patterns, interaction principles, and data visualization standards. That let design grow from 1 designer to 3 without the UI splitting.

07 · Validation & outcome

Adoption hit 73% in the first quarter.

Validation was mixed in a useful way. Dashboard layouts and filters held up. Comparables did not, until they were visual and checkable. Analysts later reported about 60% less time consolidating data into reports. Early adopters said they were seeing more opportunities in the first month because alerts actually fired.

On the live product, Pulse became the company’s market intelligence flagship. I documented components, interaction rules, and visualization standards so design could go from one person to three without the UI splitting.

08 · Reflection

I would have tested comparables before polishing role dashboards.

The process broke down where I treated “unified dashboard” as the hard part. It was not. The hard part was making a valuation something a person would defend in a meeting. We found that in tests, late enough that the comparable views had to catch up. I fixed it by putting visual, checkable properties at the center of the flow, and by writing a design system so the next screens did not invent a new language. Next time I would prototype the comparable step first, even ugly, and only then dress the executive briefing. The 73% adoption is what that correction, and a five-month 0→1, bought.
